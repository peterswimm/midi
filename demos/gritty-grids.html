How it works. Each of the 25 grid nodes is a real bytebeat one-liner from viznut's Collection of oneliner music formulas (2011-10-18) — exact and credited to their authors (viznut, tejeez, visy, rrola, ryg, red-, miiro, stephth, krcko, droid, blueberry, marmakoide, robert, Niklas_Roy, xpansive). Its 16-step curve is sampled from the formula, then Four (quarters), Backbeat (2&4) and Eighths read it at different bit offsets. The Grids-style bilinear blend of the 4 nearest programs becomes a density field; each lane's role decides where a hit can land, the field decides which land, density sets the threshold, chaos jitters it. Full density → a clean four-on-the-floor; drag the puck to morph the groove through the map.

Rhythm source. Roles imposes a danceable Four / Backbeat / Eighths skeleton (the formula only thins/accents it). Formula is the honest one — it drops the skeleton and does what Grids actually does: threshold the interpolated curve, so the pattern itself comes from the bytebeat program and morphs as you drag the map. In Formula mode each track gets its own length (L) — default 16 / 12 / 9 — so the three groove-slices are polyrhythmic, phasing against each other and only realigning every 144 pulses.

The sound is the formula too. A Bitseq hit isn't a fixed synth drum — it renders a short 1-bit grain of the current interpolated formula, so the timbre morphs as you move the map (kick / snare / hat character comes from each track's pitch). Bit voice is the sustained version: each lane a 1-bit bytebeat oscillator (the blend's top bit → a square wave), the way you'd repurpose Grids' three trigger outs as three voices in custom firmware. One puck morphs all three at once; the sliders tune each voice's pitch, Grit adds bit-noise, and the scopes show the live 1-bit waveforms. Each voice has its own Attack / Release envelope (A / R) — and A / R work in both engines: in Bit voice they shape the oscillator, in Bitseq the R sets the drum's length and A its attack. Trigger probability (P, Bit voice) hits 100% chance at ¾ of the slider; past that it ratchets — rolling each hit into 2 → 8 subdivisions, exponentially — for fills and rushes.

Chaos is one knob for mutability across all three axes at once: pattern (jitters the threshold so hits drop and appear — Grids' own randomness), timing (micro-shifts each hit off the grid), and sound (moves where each grain grabs its wavetable and detunes it, so no two hits are identical; in Bit voice it's bit-flip grit). At zero it's dead-deterministic; wind it up and the groove starts to breathe and corrode.
